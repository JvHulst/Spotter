# Spotter

Spotter is a web app for hypertrophy (muscle growth) training. It plans the weight and reps of every set and shows your progress. It runs from the iPhone home screen and works offline in the gym. Try it at https://jvhulst.github.io/Spotter/. The sections below cite the study behind each choice, and numbers without a citation are chosen by hand.

Your data stays on your phone, and neither the hosted app nor its code repository holds any of it. Optionally, Spotter keeps an encrypted copy in a private GitHub repository of your own (see Sync and privacy). A lost phone then loses nothing, and any other device with a browser stays in step with your phone.

## Getting started

Spotter opens on the Today tab, which plans your next workout. The History, Progress, Muscles and Library tabs look back at your training, and the gear icon at the top right opens Settings. Setting up Spotter on your phone takes three steps.

1. Open the app in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, and from then on open Spotter from the home screen. The home-screen app keeps its own storage, so workouts logged in a Safari tab do not appear in it.
2. To bring in your history from the Strong app, export it there (Settings, Export Strong Data) and choose Import in Spotter's Settings. Importing a newer export later adds the new workouts and updates the ones already imported, without duplicates. Exercise names that Spotter does not know become new exercises, and drop sets marked in Strong stay drop sets.
3. Log your current bodyweight on the Weigh-ins page, which the Weigh-ins link of the bodyweight card in the Progress tab opens. Later weigh-ins can also go in the bodyweight field at the top of a workout. The weigh-in with the latest date counts toward the load of pull-ups, dips and other bodyweight exercises.

To keep a copy of your data or to use a second device, set up sync on this phone next (see Sync and privacy).

## Training days

A split is a short list of training days, such as Push, Pull and Legs, that Spotter takes in turn, one per workout. The Today tab shows the plan of the next training day of the active split with a Start workout button. Below it is a panel for each workout you can start: the days of the split, your templates and an empty workout, described below. Tapping the panel of a training day or template offers Start workout, Show the plan and Edit, and the Change button switches to another split.

Splits differ in how often they train each muscle. However, at equal weekly sets per muscle, training a muscle more often does not grow it more (Schoenfeld, Grgic & Krieger 2019, J. Sports Sci. 37:1286). So the right split is the one that fits the number of days you can train each week, as listed below.

| Split | Days per week | Training days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full Body | 1–3 | A and B, each training every major muscle |
| Push / Pull | 2–4 | Push A and B with quads, Pull A and B with hamstrings |
| Upper / Lower | 4 | Upper A and B, Lower A and B |
| Push / Pull / Legs | 3 or 6 | Push, Pull, Legs |
| Upper / Lower + PPL | 5 | Upper A, Lower A, Push, Pull, Legs |

What does matter is the weekly number of hard sets per muscle, that is, sets that end close to failure. Ten or more hard sets per week grew muscle more than fewer (Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017, J. Sports Sci. 35:1073), so Spotter's weekly guide is 10 hard sets per muscle. Every working set that Spotter plans ends close to failure (see How sets are planned), so each one counts toward the guide. A few exercises with three or four sets each reach the guide with less setup and warm-up time than many exercises with one or two sets, so each training day has four or five exercises of three or four sets, ordered so that the muscles alternate.

Each training day is a list of slots, one for each exercise. A slot has a movement pattern, such as squat or row, and starts with its preferred exercise. Squat, bench press and deadlift are preferred in their slots, because strength on these three lifts is a goal of its own (see How sets are planned). Two other slots prefer the exercise that grew the muscle more in a direct comparison, namely overhead triceps extensions over pushdowns (Maeo et al. 2023, Eur. J. Sport Sci. 23:1240) and seated over lying leg curls (Maeo et al. 2021, Med. Sci. Sports Exerc. 53:825). In the other slots, the preferred exercise is the one of the slot's pattern that you logged most.

You can swap the exercise of a slot for one of its alternatives. Each exercise belongs to a pattern, and related patterns form a group. For example, the squat pattern shares a group with the leg press and lunge patterns. The exercises of all patterns in a slot's group are its alternatives. Tapping the name of an exercise in a workout opens a dropdown of them. The dropdown lists the exercises of the slot's own pattern first and those of the related patterns after, and within each part the exercises you logged most come first. Choosing one swaps it into the slot, and the slot keeps it in later workouts until you swap again.

Spotter also proposes a swap itself, but at two moments only. A planned change of exercise can help growth, whereas frequent random changes can hurt it (Kassiano et al. 2022, J. Strength Cond. Res., doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000004258). The first moment is when an exercise stalls, that is, when its last four workouts did not beat the best estimated one-rep max of the four before them (see How sets are planned). The second is after eight weeks on the same exercise. This second moment skips squat, bench press and deadlift, because strength grows most on the lift that is trained (Plotkin et al. 2023, Front. Physiol. 14:1279170). The proposed exercise is an alternative that you have logged before but not in the last eight weeks, so that Spotter can plan it from your earlier sets. When several qualify, it is the one you logged most, and when none qualifies, it is your most logged alternative. The proposal appears on the exercise's card in the workout and at the top of the dropdown. The Switch button takes it, and the Keep button stays with the current exercise and postpones every proposal for it by eight weeks.

Templates are the second kind of panel on the Today tab. A template is a list of exercises with their number of sets, each in a slot of its own whose pattern is the pattern of its exercise. The + Template button creates one in the template editor, and the Save as template button turns the current workout into one. In the template editor, each exercise also has a switch for warm-up sets and, for an isolation exercise, a choice of finisher (see During a workout). Edit on the panel of a training day opens the same editor on your own copy of that day, with the same slots and their history. The copy takes the day's place in the split until the Reset to the default button of the editor brings back the original.

Spotter tells home workouts from gym workouts by their exercises. A workout of only bodyweight exercises without added weight is a home workout, and every exercise Spotter proposes in it is such a bodyweight exercise. Any other workout is a gym workout. Spotter includes a Home template with pull-ups, push-ups, dips, inverted rows and split squats, so a workout from it is a home workout. To choose exercises as you go, start the empty workout from its panel. It counts as a gym workout until you add exercises and all of them are bodyweight exercises without added weight.

Whatever the workout, Spotter checks whether a muscle is far behind the weekly guide. It adds up the sets of each major muscle over the last 7 days and today's plan, counted as in the Muscles tab (see Progress and records). The major muscles are all muscles of the Muscles tab except the optional calves, abs and traps. When the lowest total is below 5 sets, half the guide, Spotter proposes below the exercise cards the exercise for that muscle that you logged most in the last half year. The Add button puts it in the workout. For an isolation exercise, the dropdown of alternatives also ends with a bonus section. It offers an exercise for each of the two major muscles and the one optional muscle with the fewest sets by this count that the workout does not train, so an isolation slot can train another muscle now and then.

## During a workout

In a workout, each exercise is a card with one row per set, and above the cards a field takes today's bodyweight. The label at the start of a row says what kind of set it is: W for a warm-up, a number for a working set, D for a drop set and M for myo-reps, the two finishers described below. Tapping the label changes the kind. The weight and reps are fields that show the plan and that you can type in. For an exercise with a dumbbell in each hand, enter the total weight of both dumbbells. The RIR (reps in reserve, the reps you could still have done) goes up by one with each tap, from 0 to 4+ and back to 0. Swiping a row to the left shows a Delete button. The ⋯ menu of a card swaps to any other exercise, turns the warm-up sets on or off, sets the finisher of an isolation exercise, adds a note and removes the exercise, and later workouts of the same training day or template keep these choices. The check mark at the end of a row logs the set, and a second tap undoes it.

Logging a set starts the rest timer. Rests beyond 1:30 showed no detectable extra growth (Singer et al. 2024, Front. Sports Act. Living 6:1429789), so isolation sets get 1:30. Compound sets get 2:00, since heavier multi-joint lifts take longer to recover. Supersets change these rests. The link button between two exercises makes a superset. Its sets alternate between the two exercises with 0:45 of rest in between. After each pair, the rest is the longer of the two exercises' normal rests. Some training days come with supersets, and these pair only isolation exercises, because heavy compound lifts need their full 2:00 of rest.

Isolation exercises, where failure costs little recovery, can also end with a finisher, a few extra reps close to failure in little time, and some isolation exercises of the training days come with one. Spotter has two finishers. A drop set follows the last set at once with 25% less weight for the same reps. Myo-reps follow the last set with three mini-sets of a third of the reps at the same weight, each after 0:15 of rest. Drop sets grew muscle as much as traditional sets in less time (Sødal et al. 2023, Sports Med. Open 9:66). Myo-reps are a form of rest-pause training, and in trained men rest-pause and drop-set training matched traditional sets in strength and growth (Enes et al. 2021, Appl. Physiol. Nutr. Metab. 46:1417). To change the finisher of an isolation exercise, open the ⋯ menu of its card and choose no finisher, a drop set or myo-reps. Each finisher set counts as half a set in the weekly sets per muscle. The plan of the next workout ignores them, so their low reps do not hold back the weight.

## How sets are planned

Spotter uses double progression: the reps go up first, then the weight. Each exercise has a rep range and a target RIR, such as 6–10 reps at RIR 1–2. Sets closer to failure add growth but not strength (Robinson et al. 2024, Sports Med. 54:2209), so isolation sets aim for RIR 0–1. Compound sets aim for RIR 1–2, because failure on a heavy multi-joint lift costs more fatigue and risks a failed rep under the bar.

Squat, bench press and deadlift train at 4–6 reps, so that the app also builds strength on these three lifts. Heavy loads build more maximal strength, while growth is similar across loads when sets end near failure (Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn & Krieger 2017, J. Strength Cond. Res. 31:3508). So the low range adds strength at no cost in growth. Every other exercise takes the higher range of its pattern, such as 6–10 or 10–15, so a leg press in the squat slot keeps the leg-press range. The Library page of an exercise changes its rep range and target RIR.

The stall rule in Training days, the rule for a break below, and the Progress tab use an estimated one-rep max. Spotter computes it with the Epley formula (Epley 1985, Boyd Epley Workout, Body Enterprises), counting the reps in reserve as reps that could have been done. A set of `w` kg for `r` reps at RIR `q` gives `w (1 + (r + q) / 30)`, so 100 kg for 8 reps at RIR 2 gives about 133 kg. Sets without a logged RIR, such as most sets from Strong, count as RIR 1.

Double progression works from the last workout with the exercise, by the first of these rules that applies. A step is the exercise's weight increment, such as 2.5 kg.

- Without any workout with the exercise, the plan gives the middle of the rep range and leaves the weight to you.
- After more than three weeks without the exercise, Spotter first lowers its last estimated one-rep max by 1.25% for each week beyond three, by at most 15%. It then plans the middle of the rep range at the heaviest weight that this estimate allows with the upper end of the target RIR in reserve, and no heavier than last time. Strength declines little in the first weeks without any training and more after that (Bosquet et al. 2013, Scand. J. Med. Sci. Sports 23:e140), and Spotter applies the same pattern to a break from one exercise.
- When every set at the heaviest weight of the last workout reached the top of the rep range with at least the lower end of the target RIR in reserve, the weight goes up one step. The reps are those the estimate allows at the new weight, within the rep range.
- When no set at that weight reached the bottom of the rep range, the weight goes down one step and the reps go to the bottom of the range.
- Otherwise the weight stays and each set aims for one more rep.

The rules above need a weight step. Exercises without one, such as push-ups and pull-ups, keep their weight and add reps past the top of the range. However, pull-ups, chin-ups and dips logged with added weight are filed as their weighted variant, a separate exercise that has a step and so progresses by weight. So once every set of plain pull-ups, chin-ups or dips reaches the top of the range, Spotter proposes the weighted variant on the exercise card in the next gym workout (see Training days).

During the workout, Spotter compares each logged set with its plan. When the reps done plus the RIR logged exceed the planned reps plus the upper end of the target RIR, the remaining sets get heavier. When they fall short of the planned reps plus the lower end, the remaining sets get lighter. Either way the weight moves by at most one step, so an exercise without a step keeps its weight.

Spotter also plans warm-up sets before the working sets. They do not count toward weekly sets, records or volume (weight times reps). A barbell exercise starts with 10 reps on the empty 20 kg bar. Each next warm-up adds plates toward the working weight, largest first, so you never take a plate off between warm-ups. Of the ways to load the working weight with plates of 20, 10, 5, 2.5 and 1.25 kg, Spotter picks the one whose loads on the way come closest to 50%, 70% and 85% of the working weight, for example with two 10 kg plates per side in place of one 20. When two targets fall on the same load, that load is one warm-up, so 75 kg gives the bar, 40 kg and 60 kg. Other compound exercises warm up at 50%, 70% and 85% of the working weight and other isolation exercises at 60%, each rounded down to the exercise's weight step, while a barbell isolation exercise warms up with the empty bar only. A loaded warm-up has 8 reps below 60% of the working weight, 5 below 80% and 3 above, and exercises without a weight step get no warm-ups.

## Progress and records

The Progress tab shows workouts per week, the estimated one-rep max over time of your main exercises, your bodyweight, weekly sets per muscle, weekly volume, and your personal records of the last 30 days. Sliding a finger sideways over a chart marks the value under it with its date and number, and tapping a value marks it too. The main exercises are squat, bench press, deadlift and the exercises you logged most in the last half year, until the Edit link of their card makes your own choice and order.

The bodyweight card shows each weigh-in as a dot and their trend as a line. The trend moves a tenth of the way toward each day's weight, the smoothing of John Walker's The Hacker's Diet, so that a single heavy or light day barely moves it. Above the chart are the latest trend and its change per week over the 4 weeks before the latest weigh-in. The Weigh-ins link opens the Weigh-ins page, where you add a weigh-in for any date and delete one by swiping it to the left. There you also set your phase from today on, maintain, bulk or cut, and the chart tints bulk periods green and cut periods blue.

The Muscles tab shows the sets of the last 7 days per muscle against the weekly guide of 10 sets from Training days. In both tabs, a set counts fully for an exercise's main muscle and half for the muscles it trains indirectly, such as the biceps in a row. This way of counting predicted growth best (Pelland et al. 2026, Sports Med. 56:481). However, exercises of the squat pattern count fully for the glutes as well as the quads, since squats grew the glutes as much as hip thrusts did (Plotkin et al. 2023, Front. Physiol. 14:1279170). Spotter counts the lunge pattern the same way, a choice by hand. In contrast, compound lifts count little or nothing for calves, abs and traps, so without direct work these muscles fall short of the guide. Direct work for them is a matter of taste, so Muscles lists them as optional.

Besides the records of the last 30 days in Progress, the Library shows the records of each exercise: the best estimated one-rep max, the heaviest weight, the best set and workout by volume, and, for each number of reps from 1 to 12, the heaviest weight you lifted for at least that many reps next to the weight your best estimated one-rep max predicts for exactly that many. History lists every workout with its records and the best set of each exercise, and Edit on a workout changes its date, sets and exercises.

## Sync and privacy

Sync keeps a copy of all your data in a private GitHub repository of your own. Before upload, Spotter encrypts the copy on the device with a key made from a passphrase that you choose. The passphrase never leaves the device, so GitHub cannot decrypt the data. For the same reason, Spotter cannot recover a forgotten passphrase, and without it the synced copy cannot be read. Anyone who can see the repository still sees the size of the data and the times of syncs, so keep the repository private. For those who want to check the details, the data is compressed and encrypted with AES-GCM under a 256-bit key derived from the passphrase (PBKDF2 with SHA-256 and 600,000 iterations).

To set up sync, you need a free GitHub account. On GitHub, create a new repository, for example `spotter-data`, and set it to Private. Then, under Settings, Developer settings, Personal access tokens, Fine-grained tokens, create a token with access to only that repository and set its Contents permission to Read and write. In Spotter's Settings, enter the repository as `your-github-name/spotter-data`, the token and a passphrase of at least 12 characters, such as four random words. Do this first on the device that holds your workouts. A device that connects later keeps its own workouts, but it takes over the program of the synced data (the active split, your templates and the targets of your exercises) and the settings. Other devices connect with the same repository, token and passphrase. From then on, Spotter syncs at startup, a few seconds after each change, and when it returns to the screen.

Without sync, Export backup in Settings saves all data to one file. The same Import button that reads Strong exports also reads such a backup. It adds the workouts of the backup and, after asking, its program.

## Exercise data

The exercises, movement patterns and splits are defined in data files, so changing them needs no code. `data/exercises.csv` lists the exercises with their main `muscle`, movement `pattern`, `equipment`, `increment` (the weight step in kg that the plan uses), and other names (`aliases`, separated by `|`) for matching Strong exports. Its `load` column says how bodyweight counts: `none` for external weight only, `add` for bodyweight plus the logged weight, and `subtract` for bodyweight minus assistance. `data/patterns.csv` gives each pattern its rep range (`reps_low` and `reps_high`), its `kind` (compound or isolation, which sets the target RIR, the rest and the warm-ups), its alternatives `group` and its `secondary` muscles. `data/splits.json` defines the splits, and `js/program.js` the Home template.

## Development

The app is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules without a build step. To run it locally, run `python -m http.server 8765` in the folder that contains the repository folder and open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/<repository folder>/`. `tests/progression.html` runs the unit tests in the browser. `tests/dev-load.html` fills a local copy of the app with test data and opens it. It reads a Strong export from `strong_workouts.csv` next to the repository folder, which is why the server runs in the parent folder, and the comment at its top lists its options. A service worker caches the app for offline use, so a changed file takes effect on the second launch after it was published.
