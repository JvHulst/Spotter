# Spotter

Spotter is a web app for hypertrophy (muscle growth) training. It plans the weight and reps of every set, builds strength on squat, bench press and deadlift along the way, and shows your progress. It runs from the iPhone home screen and works offline in the gym. Try it at https://jvhulst.github.io/Spotter/. The sections below cite the study behind each choice, and numbers without a citation are chosen by hand.

Your data stays on your phone, and neither the hosted app nor its code repository holds any of it. Optionally, Spotter keeps an encrypted copy in a private GitHub repository of your own (see Sync and privacy). A lost phone then loses nothing, and any other device with a browser stays in step with your phone.

## Getting started

Spotter opens on the Today tab, which plans your next workout. The History, Progress, Muscles and Library tabs look back at your training, and the gear icon at the top right opens Settings, which also holds the theme, the accent color (red, orange, blue or green) and the rest timer. Setting up Spotter on your phone takes three steps.

1. Open the app in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, and from then on open Spotter from the home screen. The home-screen app keeps its own storage, so workouts logged in a Safari tab do not appear in it.
2. To bring in your history from the Strong app, export it there (Settings, Export Strong Data) and choose Import in Spotter's Settings. Importing a newer export later adds the new workouts and updates the ones already imported, without duplicates. Exercise names that Spotter does not know become new exercises, and drop sets marked in Strong stay drop sets.
3. Spotter counts the weigh-in with the latest date in the load of pull-ups, dips and other bodyweight exercises, so log your current bodyweight on the Weigh-ins page (Progress tab, Weigh-ins link of the bodyweight card). To bring in many earlier weigh-ins at once, choose Import in Settings and pick a CSV file with the columns `date` (written as 2025-12-21) and `kg`. Later weigh-ins can also go in the bodyweight field at the top of a workout.

To keep a copy of your data or to use a second device, set up sync on this phone next (see Sync and privacy).

## Training days

Each workout follows a training day of your split, one of your templates, or nothing at all. A split is a short list of training days, such as Push, Pull and Legs, that Spotter takes in turn, and only a workout of a training day moves the split on to its next day. The Today tab shows the plan of the next training day of the active split with a Start workout button. Below it, under Or do another workout, is a panel for each other workout you can start: the other days of the split, your templates and an empty workout, described below. Tapping the panel of a training day or template offers Start workout, Show the plan and Edit. The Change button, at the top of the Today tab, switches to another split.

Choose the split that fits the number of days you can train each week, as listed below. Splits differ in how often they train each muscle. However, at equal weekly sets per muscle, training a muscle more often does not grow it more (Schoenfeld, Grgic & Krieger 2019, J. Sports Sci. 37:1286), so where several splits fit, pick the one you like.

| Split | Days per week | Training days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full Body | 1–3 | A and B, each training the whole body |
| Push / Pull | 2–4 | Push A and B with quads, Pull A and B with hamstrings |
| Upper / Lower | 4 | Upper A and B, Lower A and B |
| Push / Pull / Legs (PPL) | 3 or 6 | Push, Pull, Legs |
| Upper / Lower + PPL | 5 | Upper A, Lower A, Push, Pull, Legs |

Growth depends instead on the weekly number of hard sets per muscle, that is, sets that end close to failure. Ten or more hard sets per week grew muscle more than fewer than five (Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017, J. Sports Sci. 35:1073), so Spotter's weekly guide is 10 hard sets per muscle. Every working set that Spotter plans ends close to failure (see How sets are planned), so each one counts toward the guide.

Each training day has four or five exercises of three or four sets, because a few exercises with several sets reach the guide with less setup and warm-up time than many exercises with one or two sets. The exception is the leg compounds (squat, leg press and hinge exercises), which get three sets, a choice by hand since a heavy leg set costs more recovery than an upper-body set. The exercises are ordered so that the muscles alternate, which lets one muscle rest while the next one works. Each exercise sits in a slot, which has a movement pattern, such as squat or row, and holds its preferred exercise until you swap it. Squat, bench press and deadlift are preferred in their slots, because besides growth Spotter aims to build strength on these three lifts (see How sets are planned). The slots for triceps extensions and leg curls prefer the variant that grew the muscle more in a direct comparison, namely overhead extensions over pushdowns (Maeo et al. 2023, Eur. J. Sport Sci. 23:1240) and seated over lying leg curls (Maeo et al. 2021, Med. Sci. Sports Exerc. 53:825). In the other slots, the preferred exercise is the one of the slot's pattern that you logged most, and the most common variant before you have logged any.

You can swap the exercise of a slot for one of its alternatives, or for any exercise through the ⋯ menu of its card (see During a workout). Each exercise belongs to a pattern, and related patterns form a group. For example, the squat pattern shares a group with the leg press and lunge patterns. The exercises of all patterns in a slot's group are its alternatives. Tapping the name of an exercise in a workout opens a dropdown of them. The dropdown lists the exercises of the slot's own pattern first and those of the related patterns after, and within each part the exercises you logged most come first. Choosing one swaps it into the slot, and the slot keeps it in later workouts until you swap again.

Spotter also proposes a swap itself, but at two moments only. Spotter proposes swaps this rarely because a planned change of exercise can help growth, whereas frequent random changes can hurt it (Kassiano et al. 2022, J. Strength Cond. Res., doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000004258). The first moment is when an exercise stalls, that is, when the best estimated one-rep max of its last four workouts is no higher than the best of the four before them (see How sets are planned). This applies to every exercise, since a variant can restart the progress of a lift that no longer moves. The second moment is after eight weeks on the same exercise, and it skips squat, bench press and deadlift, because strength is a goal on these lifts and it grows most on the lift that is trained (Plotkin et al. 2023, Front. Physiol. 14:1279170). The proposed exercise is an alternative that you have logged before but not in the last eight weeks, so that Spotter can plan it from your earlier sets. When several qualify, it is the one you logged most. When none qualifies, it is the alternative you logged most, or the most common one when you have logged none. The proposal appears on the exercise's card in the workout and at the top of the dropdown. The Switch button takes it. The Keep button stays with the current exercise, and Spotter then proposes no swap for that exercise for eight weeks.

Your templates are the second kind of panel under Or do another workout, and they work differently from the training days. A training day is adaptive: Spotter picks its exercises, proposes swaps and keeps the choices you make in it. A template is fixed: it is a list of exercises with their number of sets, warm-up switches, finishers and supersets exactly as you saved them, and a workout from it follows that list. Spotter proposes no swaps and no extra exercises in it, and nothing you change during the workout is stored in the template. Only Save as template and Update template change it. Its weights and reps still follow the plan of each exercise (see How sets are planned). Each exercise sits in a slot of its own pattern. The + Template button creates a template in the template editor, and the Save as template button turns the current workout into one. In the template editor, each exercise has a grip that reorders the exercises by dragging, also on the page of the dashboard exercises in the Progress tab, and a switch for warm-up sets and, for an isolation exercise, a finisher (see During a workout). Edit on the panel of a training day opens the same editor on your own copy of that day. The copy keeps the day's slots, so they keep the exercises you last chose in them, it takes the day's place in the split, and it is fixed like any template until the Reset to the default button of the editor brings back the adaptive original. The third kind of panel starts an empty workout, to which you add exercises as you go.

Spotter includes one template, Home, with pull-ups, push-ups, dips, inverted rows and split squats. A workout from it is a home workout, and so is any workout of only bodyweight exercises without added weight. In a home workout, Spotter proposes only such bodyweight exercises, both as swaps and as added exercises (see below). Any other workout is a gym workout. An empty workout counts as a gym workout until you have added exercises and all of them are bodyweight exercises without added weight.

Whatever the workout, Spotter checks whether a muscle is far behind the weekly guide. It adds up the sets of each major muscle over the last 7 days and today's plan, counted as in the Muscles tab (see Progress and records). The major muscles are all muscles of the Muscles tab except calves, abs and traps, which the tab lists as optional. When the lowest total is below 5 sets, half the guide, Spotter proposes an exercise for that muscle below the exercise cards, namely the one you logged most in the last half year. The Add button puts it in the workout. For an isolation exercise, the dropdown of alternatives also ends with a bonus section. Among the muscles that the workout does not train, Spotter takes the two major muscles and the one optional muscle with the fewest sets by this count, and the bonus section offers your most logged isolation exercise for each. Choosing one is a swap like any other, so an isolation slot can move on to another muscle.

## During a workout

In a workout, each exercise is a card with one row per set, and above the cards a field takes today's bodyweight. The label at the start of a row says what kind of set it is: W for a warm-up, a number for a working set, D for a drop set and M for myo-reps, the two finishers described below. Tapping the label opens a list of the set types to choose from. The weight and reps are fields that show the plan and that you can type in. A field that you have not typed in and whose set is not logged shows its value in gray. Typing a value into a working set also sets the gray fields of the working sets after it, so changing the first set of four equal sets changes all four, and each later set keeps the value that you typed into it. Warm-ups, drop sets and myo sets are not part of this chain. Logging a set turns its fields white. To change the weight of the sets that are still to come, change the next one. For an exercise with a dumbbell in each hand, enter the total weight of both dumbbells. Tapping the RIR (reps in reserve, the reps you could still have done) opens a list from 0 (to failure) to 4+. Swiping a row to the left shows a Delete button. The grip at the start of a card's header reorders the exercises: while you hold it, the cards shrink to one line each, and you drag the card to its place and release. The ⋯ menu of a card opens the exercise's page, swaps to any other exercise, turns the warm-up sets on or off, adds a note and removes the exercise. In a training day, later workouts keep these choices. The check mark at the end of a row logs the set, and a second tap undoes it. The Minimize button next to Finish returns to the rest of the app while the workout continues. A bar above the tabs shows the workout, its time and the rest countdown, and tapping it returns to the workout. After the last set, Finish opens a summary with the minutes, working sets and volume of the workout, and confetti when a squat, bench press or deadlift set the best estimated one-rep max so far.

Logging a set starts the rest timer, and its length follows the set that comes next. Before a warm-up it is the warm-up rest, so also after the last working set of an exercise when the next exercise starts with warm-ups. Otherwise it is the rest of the exercise just trained, so also after the last warm-up. When the rest ends, Spotter plays a sound, which you choose in Settings (a boxing bell, FAHHH, a vine boom and more, each played once when chosen). Rests beyond 1:30 showed no detectable extra growth (Singer et al. 2024, Front. Sports Act. Living 6:1429789), so isolation sets get 1:30. Compound sets get 2:00, since heavier multi-joint lifts take longer to recover, and the Library page of an exercise changes its rest time. Supersets change these rests. The link button between two exercises makes a superset. Its working sets alternate between the two exercises. The rest after a set of the first exercise is 0:45, and after a set of the second it is the longer of the two exercises' normal rests, so the timer starts when the other exercise is ready for its next working set. If the other exercise still has warm-ups, they come first and the rest is the warm-up rest. Drop sets and myo-reps stay with their exercise, and a warm-up does not hand over to the partner. Some training days come with supersets, and these pair only isolation exercises, because heavy compound lifts need their full 2:00 of rest.

Isolation exercises, where failure costs little recovery, can also end with a finisher, a few extra reps close to failure in little time, and some isolation exercises of the training days come with one. A finisher is a set type like a warm-up, chosen in the list that opens from the label of a set. Spotter has two finishers. A drop set follows its set at once, after 0:10 of rest, with 25% less weight for the same reps. Myo-reps follow with three mini-sets of a third of the reps at the same weight, each after 0:15 of rest. Drop sets grew muscle as much as traditional sets in less time (Sødal et al. 2023, Sports Med. Open 9:66). Myo-reps are a form of rest-pause training, and in trained men rest-pause and drop-set training matched traditional sets in strength and growth (Enes et al. 2021, Appl. Physiol. Nutr. Metab. 46:1417). Marking a set of an isolation exercise as a drop set or myo-reps adds that finisher to the exercise in later workouts of the training day, and marking none removes it. Each finisher set counts as half a set in the weekly sets per muscle. The plan of the next workout ignores them, so their low reps do not hold back the weight.

## How sets are planned

Spotter uses double progression: the reps go up until they reach the top of the exercise's rep range, and then the weight goes up. The rep range and the target RIR depend on the category of the exercise, so swapping squats for leg presses in a slot also changes them. The table lists the defaults per category, which you can change in Settings.

| Category | Exercises | Reps | Target RIR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy compounds | compound exercises with a barbell, such as squat, bench press, deadlift and barbell rows | 5–8 | 1–2 |
| Other compounds | compound exercises on machines and cables, with dumbbells or with bodyweight | 8–12 | 0–1 |
| Isolations | exercises for one joint, such as curls, lateral raises and leg extensions | 10–20 | 0–1 |

The ranges and target RIRs in the table rest on two findings on strength and growth. Heavy loads build more maximal strength, while growth is similar across loads when sets end near failure (Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn & Krieger 2017, J. Strength Cond. Res. 31:3508). So the low range of the heavy compounds builds strength on squat, bench press, deadlift and the other barbell lifts at no cost in growth. The other categories, where strength is not a goal, use higher ranges, which by the same study grow muscle as well. The target RIR follows from the second finding, that sets closer to failure add growth but not strength (Robinson et al. 2024, Sports Med. 54:2209). Spotter assumes that failure costs little outside the heavy compounds, so the other categories may go to failure (RIR 0) for the extra growth. Under a heavy bar, however, failure costs much more fatigue and risks getting stuck under the bar. So heavy compounds stop one or two reps short, which gives up a little growth but no strength.

The stall rule in Training days, the rule for a break below, and the Progress tab use an estimated one-rep max. Spotter computes it with the Epley formula (Epley 1985, Boyd Epley Workout, Body Enterprises), counting the reps in reserve as reps that could have been done. A set of `w` kg for `r` reps at RIR `q` gives `w (1 + (r + q) / 30)`, so 100 kg for 8 reps at RIR 2 gives about 133 kg. Sets without a logged RIR, such as most sets from Strong, count as RIR 1.

Double progression works from the last workout with the exercise, by the first of these rules that applies. A step is the exercise's weight increment, such as 2.5 kg, and the exercise's Library page changes it.

- Without any workout with the exercise, the plan gives the middle of the rep range and leaves the weight to you.
- After more than three weeks without the exercise, Spotter first lowers its last estimated one-rep max by 1.25% for each week beyond three, by at most 15%. It then plans the middle of the rep range at the heaviest weight that this estimate allows with the upper end of the target RIR in reserve, and no heavier than last time. Strength declines little in the first weeks without any training and more after that (Bosquet et al. 2013, Scand. J. Med. Sci. Sports 23:e140), and Spotter applies the same pattern to a break from one exercise.
- When every set at the heaviest weight of the last workout reached the top of the rep range with at least the lower end of the target RIR in reserve, the weight goes up one step. The reps are those the estimate allows at the new weight, within the rep range.
- When no set at that weight reached the bottom of the rep range, the weight goes down one step and the reps go to the bottom of the range.
- Otherwise the weight stays and each set aims for one more rep.

The rules above need a weight step. Exercises without one, such as push-ups and pull-ups, keep their weight and add reps past the top of the range. However, pull-ups, chin-ups and dips logged with added weight are filed as their weighted variant, a separate exercise that has a step and so progresses by weight. So once every set of plain pull-ups, chin-ups or dips reaches the top of the range, Spotter proposes the weighted variant on the exercise card in the next gym workout (see Training days).

During the workout, Spotter compares each logged set with its plan. When the reps done plus the RIR logged exceed the planned reps plus the upper end of the target RIR, the remaining sets get heavier. When they fall short of the planned reps plus the lower end, the remaining sets get lighter. Either way the weight moves by at most one step, so an exercise without a step keeps its weight.

Spotter also plans warm-up sets before the working sets. They do not count toward weekly sets, records or volume (weight times reps). A barbell exercise starts with 10 reps on the empty 20 kg bar. Each next warm-up adds plates toward the working weight, largest first, so you never take a plate off between warm-ups. Of the ways to load the working weight with plates of 20, 10, 5, 2.5 and 1.25 kg, Spotter picks the one whose loads on the way come closest to 50%, 70% and 85% of the working weight, for example with two 10 kg plates per side in place of one 20. When two targets fall on the same load, that load is one warm-up, so 75 kg gives the bar, 40 kg and 60 kg. Other compound exercises warm up at 50%, 70% and 85% of the working weight, each rounded down to the exercise's weight step. Isolation exercises get no warm-ups, since their loads are too light to need a ramp. A loaded warm-up has 8 reps below 60% of the working weight, 5 below 80% and 3 above, whatever the rep range of the exercise, and exercises without a weight step get no warm-ups.

## Progress and records

The Progress tab shows workouts per week, the estimated one-rep max over time of your main exercises, your bodyweight, weekly sets per muscle, weekly volume, and your personal records of the last 30 days. By default, the main exercises are squat, bench press, deadlift and the exercises you logged most in the last half year, eight in all. The Edit link of their card sets your own choice and order. Sliding a finger sideways over a chart marks the value under it with its date and number, and tapping a value marks it too.

The bodyweight card shows each weigh-in as a dot and their trend as a line. The trend is the moving average of The Hacker's Diet (Walker 1991): each day it moves a tenth of the way toward that day's weight, and over a gap of several days it moves as far as those days together would. So a single heavy or light day barely moves it. Above the chart are the latest trend and its change per week over the four weeks before the latest weigh-in. On the Weigh-ins page (see Getting started) you add a weigh-in for any date and delete one by swiping it to the left. There you also set your phase (maintain, bulk or cut) from today on. The phase only marks the chart, which tints bulk periods green and cut periods blue.

The Muscles tab shows the sets of the last 7 days per muscle against the weekly guide of 10 sets from Training days. In both tabs, a set counts fully for an exercise's main muscle and half for the muscles it trains indirectly, such as the biceps in a row. This way of counting predicted growth best (Pelland et al. 2026, Sports Med. 56:481). However, exercises of the squat pattern count fully for the glutes as well as the quads, since squats grew the glutes as much as hip thrusts did (Plotkin et al. 2023, Front. Physiol. 14:1279170). Spotter counts the lunge pattern the same way, a choice by hand. In contrast, compound lifts count little or nothing for calves, abs and traps, so without direct work these muscles fall short of the guide. Direct work for them is a matter of taste, so Muscles lists them as optional.

Besides the records of the last 30 days in Progress, the Library shows the records of each exercise: the best estimated one-rep max, the heaviest weight, the best set and workout by volume, and, for each number of reps from 1 to 12, the heaviest weight you lifted for at least that many reps next to the weight your best estimated one-rep max predicts for exactly that many. History lists every workout with its records and the best set of each exercise. Opening a workout shows, for each exercise, its best estimated one-rep max and its volume, and for each working set its estimated one-rep max. A value that is a personal record of that workout is colored and marked PR. Edit on a workout changes its date, sets and exercises.

## Sync and privacy

Sync keeps a copy of all your data in a private GitHub repository of your own. Before upload, Spotter encrypts the copy on the device with a key made from a passphrase that you choose. The passphrase never leaves the device, so GitHub cannot decrypt the data. For the same reason, Spotter cannot recover a forgotten passphrase, and without it the synced copy cannot be read. Anyone who can see the repository still sees the size of the data and the times of syncs, so keep the repository private. For those who want to check the details, the data is compressed and encrypted with AES-GCM under a 256-bit key derived from the passphrase (PBKDF2 with SHA-256 and 600,000 iterations).

To set up sync, you need a free GitHub account. On GitHub, create a new repository, for example `spotter-data`, and set it to Private. Then, under Settings, Developer settings, Personal access tokens, Fine-grained tokens, create a token with access to only that repository and set its Contents permission to Read and write. In Spotter's Settings, enter the repository as `your-github-name/spotter-data`, the token and a passphrase of at least 12 characters, such as four random words. Do this first on the device that holds your workouts. A device that connects later keeps its own workouts, but it takes over the program of the synced data (the active split, your templates and the targets of your exercises) and the settings. Other devices connect with the same repository, token and passphrase. From then on, Spotter syncs at startup, a few seconds after each change, and when it returns to the screen.

Without sync, Export backup in Settings saves all data to one file. The same Import button that reads Strong exports and weigh-in files also reads such a backup. It adds the workouts of the backup and, after asking, its program.

## Exercise data

The exercises, movement patterns and splits are defined in data files, so changing them needs no code. `data/exercises.csv` lists the exercises with their main `muscle`, movement `pattern`, `equipment`, `increment` (the weight step in kg that the plan uses), and other names (`aliases`, separated by `|`) for matching Strong exports. Its `load` column says how bodyweight counts: `none` for external weight only, `add` for bodyweight plus the logged weight, and `subtract` for bodyweight minus assistance. `data/patterns.csv` describes each pattern. Its `kind`, compound or isolation, sets the default rest time, and together with the exercise's `equipment` it sets the category (see How sets are planned) and the warm-ups. Its `group` of related patterns sets the alternatives, and its `secondary` muscles are the ones its sets count half for, or the share given after a colon (see Progress and records). `data/splits.json` defines the splits, and `js/program.js` the Home template. `data/cues.json` holds the steps of each exercise and `img/ex/` its two photos, shown on the exercise page in the Library. Both come from the public-domain free-exercise-db and are rebuilt with `python tools/build_cues.py` (needs Pillow), which lists in `MATCH` the database entry that each exercise uses. A new exercise in `exercises.csv` needs an entry there.

## Development

The app is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules without a build step. To run it locally, run `python -m http.server 8765` in the folder that contains the repository folder and open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/<repository folder>/`. `tests/progression.html` runs the unit tests in the browser. The module files are served by the service worker from its cache, so while developing, unregister the service worker in the browser's developer tools or open the app in a private window. `tests/dev-load.html` fills a local copy of the app with test data and opens it. It reads a Strong export from `strong_workouts.csv` next to the repository folder, which is why the server runs in the parent folder, and the comment at its top lists its options. A service worker caches the app for offline use, so a changed file takes effect on the second launch after it was published.
